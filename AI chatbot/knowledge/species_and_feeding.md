# Species context and feeding management

Fish species, strain, age, production system, and local climate materially
change disease risk and acceptable water conditions. Generic ranges must not
replace farm-specific standards.

## Tilapia

Tilapia are warm-water fish, but temperature tolerance differs among species,
strains, and life stages. Chilling and rapid temperature change can suppress
feeding and increase susceptibility to opportunistic infection. Common
investigations for sick tilapia include dissolved oxygen, temperature,
ammonia/nitrite, crowding, recent handling, skin and gill parasites, bacterial
infection, and regionally important viruses. A photograph cannot rule in or out
Tilapia lake virus or other systemic disease; laboratory testing is needed.

## Carp

Carp production includes multiple species with different requirements.
External parasites, gill injury, bacterial ulcers, poor oxygen, and viral
diseases can produce overlapping lethargy, respiratory distress, or skin
lesions. Unexplained increasing mortality or hemorrhagic signs warrant movement
controls and professional investigation rather than empiric medication.

## Catfish

Catfish signs such as reduced feeding, erratic swimming, skin lesions, and gill
distress can arise from environment, handling, parasites, or infection.
Scaleless skin may respond differently to some chemicals, so a treatment that
is tolerated by another fish species may not be safe for catfish.

## Feeding practice

Use a complete feed appropriate to species and life stage. Store feed cool,
dry, off the floor, protected from pests, and within its usable storage period.
Rancid, moldy, or nutrient-degraded feed can impair performance and health.

Base ration on biomass, temperature, fish size, and observed appetite. Uneaten
feed increases organic load, ammonia production, bacterial growth, and oxygen
demand. A sudden appetite decrease is an early warning: check water quality and
behavior before continuing the normal ration. During acute low oxygen or severe
stress, temporarily withholding feed is often safer than adding more oxygen
demand, but the response must fit the species and production plan.

Track feed offered, mortalities, growth, and feed conversion. A trend change can
reveal a developing water-quality or health problem before obvious lesions
appear.
